1. WHAT THIS TOOL DOES (AND DOESN'T DO)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
1.1 What it does
The calculator simulates a full party rotation over time and reports what
each hit deals, the total damage, and the overall DPS. It models every
character, weapon, and artifact set shipped with this version, including
their triggered effects (extra hits, buff windows, stack decay, cooldowns,
and reaction damage).

The simulation is event-based: each ability schedules its hits at fixed
offsets from the cast, the timeline resolves them in order, and the buffs
for every hit are recomputed from the exact party state at that moment.
Because of that, changing a rotation's timings can change the damage of
every subsequent hit. On-cast (or on-button-press) effects are considered
before the first hit of the action.

Ability hit timings are based on my own setup's performance, including
FPS and ping. All timeline data is taken from personal tests on the
training dummy, and the available preset trials take into account my
personal timings as well.

All calculations are presented as transparently as possible via the
timeline table in Tab 4. Any mistakes spotted can be sent to me, and
I'll gladly try to fix them.

1.2 What it doesn't do
- It does not calculate particle generation or Energy Recharge
  requirements for the party.
- It does not calculate events based on the distance to the enemy.
- It does not consider attack frame counts or hitlag; it's simply a
  simulation of scheduled hit events.
- It does not calculate AoE damage (all calculations assume single target).
- It does not run random trials with RNG rolls. All damage numbers are
  mathematical averages (unless everyone has 100% Crit Rate).

2. QUICK START
----------------------------------------------------------------------
If you want to jump right in, here is the fastest way to get a simulation
running:

  1. Fill the four party slots in Tab 1 and click "Confirm Party
     Configuration". Artifacts are optional. Once confirmed, you can
     check everyone's off-field stats on their stat cards.
  2. Set the enemy level and resistances in Tab 2 and click "Confirm Enemy
     Configuration". Check the updated enemy card.
  3. Pick a trial from the dropdown in Tab 3 and press "Run Trial
     Simulation".
  4. Review your results, graphs, and the full event timeline in Tab 4.

3. TAB 1 - TEAM & GEAR SETUP
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Four party slots are displayed. For each slot, configure:

3.1 Character, Level, Constellation, Weapon, and Talents
  Character       The character for that slot.
                  Available: Mizuki, Odette, Anastasya, Aether, Faruzan,
                  Sucrose, Diona.
  Level           60 / 70 / 80 / 90 / 95 / 100
                  (corresponds to A4 / A4 / A5 / A6 / A7 / A8 respectively).
                  Level 60 (A4) is the minimum to guarantee all characters'
                  ascension passives are unlocked.
  Constellation   C0 / C1 / C2 / C3 / C4 / C5 / C6.
                  Note: C3 and C5 are currently decorative in this dropdown;
                  they do not automatically bump talent levels in the app,
                  so enter your boosted talent levels manually.
                  Unavailable constellations are not implemented.
  Weapon          Only weapons that the selected character can equip are
                  listed.
  Refinement      R1 / R2 / R3 / R4 / R5.
  Talent Levels   Normal / Skill / Burst (e.g. 1 / 13 / 13).
                  Remember to include C3 and C5 bonuses here if applicable.

3.2 Artifacts and Substat Standards
  Artifact set and pieces:
    Choose the artifact set, then set each piece's main stat, rarity, and
    up to four substats. The Flower and Feather pieces have fixed main stats
    (HP and ATK respectively).

  Substat standard (carries only):
    Manual                    You enter all main stats and substats yourself.
    100% RV (Crit Value)      The best-case crit-value distribution for that
                              character (assuming average roll values); main
                              stats and substats are automatically generated.
    KQM Standards (40 rolls)  A more realistic 40-roll standard based on the
                              KeqingMains framework:
                              https://compendium.keqingmains.com/

    Both standards lock the main stats and substats, modeling crit as a
    unified Crit Value (CV) pool. They are only available for characters
    that have a defined standard.
    Note: Energy Recharge requirements are currently NOT taken into account
    in either 100% RV or KQM Standards mode.

3.3 Confirming Configuration & Off-Field Stats
  Press CONFIRM PARTY CONFIGURATION when all four slots are ready. The
  stat cards appear under the section header and stay visible while you
  tweak slots and artifacts below. The card stats mirror the off-field
  stats displayed in-game (so Aether, for example, doesn't receive bonus
  stats from each resonated element while off-field).

  If you change gear, substats, or the substat standard after confirming,
  the app warns you and hides the cards until you confirm again. However,
  running a trial always uses your active inputs, confirmed or not.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
4. TAB 2 - ENEMY CONFIGURATION
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Set the enemy level and its base resistance for each element (Pyro, Cryo,
Hydro, Electro, Anemo, Geo, Dendro, and Physical), then click CONFIRM
ENEMY CONFIGURATION.

Resistance shred from character buffs, passives, and artifact sets (like
Viridescent Venerer) will be applied dynamically on top of these base
values when damage is calculated. You can inspect the final resistance
multiplier for every hit in the detailed timeline table in Tab 4.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
5. TAB 3 - ROTATION SETUP
----------------------------------------------------------------------
5.1 Running a trial
  Pick a trial from the dropdown. Preset trials only appear when the party
  matches the exact team they were designed for; custom trials are always
  visible.

  The TRIAL BREAKDOWN expander shows the description, the end mode, the
  trial duration, and every rotation with its field time and individual
  action steps:

      Rotation #1: Standard Mizuki-Faruzan Loop [19.702s] (Repeated 6x)
        Step 1: Aether EN1 (Aether - 1.23s field time)
        Step 2: Faruzan Q (Faruzan - 1.41s field time)
        ...

  END MODE:
    Time       The trial stops strictly when the configured duration expires.
    Last swap  The trial ends when the last scheduled character swaps out.
               The duration shown is the sum of all field times.

  Press RUN TRIAL SIMULATION to execute the run. Each completed run is
  automatically saved to the history log in Tab 4.
  If a rotation attempts to cast an ability before its cooldown has reset,
  the simulation stops immediately with a Cooldown Error instead of
  generating invalid results.

5.2 Field sequences
  A field sequence represents one character's time on-field: which
  abilities they use, when each ability starts (in seconds relative to
  when they swap in), and their total on-field duration. You can build one
  under "Create Custom Field Sequence":
    - Enter the sequence name, the character, and their total field
      (swap-out) time.
    - Select an ability and its start time offset, then click "Add".
    - Repeat for all abilities cast during that visit, then click "Save
      Field Sequence".

  Only abilities belonging to characters currently in your party are
  available. Note that engine-injected procs (extra triggered hits,
  coordinated attacks, passives) cannot be added manually—they fire
  automatically based on their own in-game conditions.

5.3 Rotations
  A rotation is an ordered sequence of character visits (field sequences)
  plus a repetition count. In "Create Custom Rotation", add your saved
  field sequences one by one.

  The same field sequence can be added multiple times. This is especially
  handy for patterns like "Burst every other rotation": just include both
  the Burst variant and the non-Burst variant inside one rotation and set
  it to repeat. Use the arrow buttons to reorder visits and the "X" button
  to remove one, then click "Save Rotation".

5.4 Trials assembly
  A trial combines one or more rotations and defines how the simulation
  concludes. In the trial assembly area:
    - Set the name and description.
    - Choose the End Mode (Time or Last swap), setting the duration if
      using Time.
    - Add rotations along with their repetition counts using "Add Rotation
      to Trial".
    - Click "Create Trial".

  You can also click "Load selected trial into editor" to copy an existing
  trial's rotations (including the built-in presets) into the editor so you
  can tweak and repurpose them without starting from scratch.

6. TAB 4 - SIMULATION RESULTS & HISTORY
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Every executed run is recorded in Tab 4 alongside the exact party, gear,
enemy, and trial configurations used. The most recent run is expanded by
default. Past runs can be renamed, deleted, or exported as standalone build
files.

6.1 Overview metrics
  Total Damage    The sum of average damage across every single hit landed
                  during the trial.
  Overall DPS     Total Damage divided by the actual trial duration.
  Trial Duration  The exact simulation time window: the configured limit
                  for a Time trial, or the cumulative field time for a
                  Last swap trial.

6.2 Charts & visualizations
  Alongside the summary numbers, the app displays four visual charts to
  help you analyze team performance:
    - Damage by Element (Pie Chart): Shows elemental distribution.
    - Damage by Hit Type / Category (Pie Chart): Breaks down damage by
      type of damage.
    - Damage by Character (Pie Chart): Compares each character's direct
      damage output.
      Note: This chart excludes Stellar Reaction damage so that credit
      attribution doesn't artificially inflate a character's personal
      share over their teammates (see Section 7 for how reaction shares
      work).
    - DPS Timeline (Bar Chart): Shows per-second damage output across the
      entire duration of the trial. This is especially useful for
      identifying your burst spikes and timing your team's highest DPS
      windows to coincide with boss vulnerability or down phases.

6.3 The Rotation Timeline table
  The timeline table provides a complete, chronological log of every event
  in the trial. At present, events consist of character swaps and damage
  instances. (Buff activations and stack gains aren't listed as standalone
  events, but their status is snapshotted on every hit).

  The main purpose of this table is total transparency: it lets you verify
  exactly how every single number was calculated. By following the game's
  underlying rules, the table tracks active buffs, debuffs, and stack
  counts at the exact millisecond of impact, feeding those values directly
  into the damage formula.

  - The standard view displays only the most essential columns, keeping it
    clean and easy to read at a glance.
  - Ticking "Show detailed columns" expands the table into a full audit
    view. Because this view contains a wealth of data, downloading the
    table and opening it in spreadsheet software (like Excel or Google
    Sheets) is highly recommended for deep analysis.

6.4 Detailed timeline column blocks
  To make the damage math as clear as possible, the detailed table is
  organized into several main blocks:

  - Description block [Time Elapsed / Active Character / Event Owner / Action Name]:
    Covers basic event metadata: the timestamp, who is currently on-field,
    who owns the action, and the specific skill/attack name.

  - Elemental block [Target's Cryo Aura / Dmg Element / Elemental App / Reaction Triggered]:
    Tracks elements and reactions triggered by the hit. The app currently
    tracks Cryo auras. If an Anemo attack hits but cannot trigger Swirl
    because no Cryo aura is present, the app flags it as "No Aura". This
    makes it easier to spot missed swirls in the rotation.

  - Stats block [HP / ATK / DEF / EM / CR% / CD% / CV]:
    The event owner's combat stats snapshotted at that moment, including
    all active stat buffs. (CR% and CD% are hidden when using the Crit
    Value standard mode, as crit is tracked as combined CV instead).

  - Multipliers block [Base DMG / Quills / Bonus Mult / Elev Mult / Crit Mult / Def Mult / Res Mult]:
    Displays the core multipliers that determine the hit's damage.
    (Note: Multipliers are currently hidden for Stellar Reaction damage;
    see Section 7 for why).
      * Base DMG: The talent motion value multiplied by the scaling stat
        (e.g. ATK).
      * Quills: Additive base damage from buffs. For standard damage,
        this adds directly to Base DMG.
      * Bonus Mult: Sum of elemental/stellar damage bonuses, EM scaling,
        attack-type bonuses, etc.
      * Elev Mult: The Elevation Multiplier, currently used for Stellar
        Glimmer damage (see Section 7).
      * Crit Mult: The statistical expected multiplier from critical hits
        (1 + CR * CD). In CV mode, this uses the optimal 1:2 crit
        distribution formula.
      * Def Mult: Defense mitigation factor based on attacker level vs.
        enemy level.
      * Res Mult: Enemy resistance factor based on base elemental resistance
        minus resistance shred.

  - Damage Output block [Avg DMG / Non-Crit DMG / Crit DMG / Effective Avg DMG]:
    The final calculated damage numbers. "Effective Avg DMG" shows the
    damage output before applying the enemy's resistance multiplier, which
    is useful for testing raw damage thresholds against boss Effective HP
    pools.

  - Buffs block:
    Displays the active state of all major buffs relevant to the trial. If
    a buff utilizes stacks, its current stack count is displayed instead.
    If there is any buff you want tracked in its own column that isn't
    currently shown, feel free to ask me and I can add it.

7. DAMAGE CATEGORIES & STELLAR SWIRL MECHANICS
----------------------------------------------------------------------
7.1 The three damage categories
The calculator divides damage instances into three main categories based on
the underlying formulas and multipliers they use:

  1. Standard Damage:
     The conventional damage model used by most abilities in the game. It
     is mitigated by enemy defense (Def Mult) and affected by elemental
     resistances (Res Mult). Additive buffs ("quills") are added directly
     to Base DMG before bonus multipliers.
     Standard damage is further categorized by attack type: Normal,
     Charged, Plunge, Skill, and Burst. Elemental damage bonuses apply
     within the Bonus Multiplier. Currently, there is no Elevation
     Multiplier for standard damage.

  2. Stellar Damage (Stellar Direct Damage):
     Known as "stellar direct damage" in the wiki. This damage type
     completely ignores enemy defense. Additive quills do not scale with
     the Bonus Multiplier here. The Bonus Multiplier displayed in the
     timeline table already includes the reaction base damage multiplier
     (such as from Stellar Jubilee passives).

  3. Stellar Reaction Damage:
     Known as "stellar indirect damage" in the wiki. The most intricate
     damage type in the app, covering Stellar Swirls and
     Stellar Vortex explosions. Because multiple party members contribute
     to each reaction and their individual contributions can independently
     crit, the final damage is calculated through a joint probability
     distribution. See Section 7.2 below for the complete breakdown.
     Quills for this category (currently only Mizuki C1) are added after
     contributor shares are distributed, scaling with the quill owner's crit
     stats, without applying bonus multipliers.

7.2 Stellar Swirl & Vortex mechanics
When characters possessing the Stellar Linchpin effect are present in the
party, the team's standard Swirl reaction transforms into Stellar Swirl:

  - Stellar Glimmer mode:
    Triggering a Stellar Swirl puts the entire party into Stellar Glimmer
    mode for 8 seconds (currently assumed to be permanently active in the
    sim), enabling their special Stellar Swirl mechanics and buffs.

  - Natural Crits:
    Unlike standard Swirls (which rely purely on character level and EM and
    cannot crit), Stellar Swirls and Vortex explosions can naturally crit
    based on the contributing characters' Crit Rate and Crit DMG stats.

  - Two Damage Instances:
    Stellar Swirl damage is delivered in two separate instances:
      1. The Swirl portion:
         Triggered when Anemo is applied to a Cryo aura, consuming half of
         the attack's gauge. Unlike the standard Swirl damage, it deals Anemo
         damage and can critically strike.
      2. The Stellar Vortex explosion portion:
         Triggering a Stellar Swirl immediately spawns a Lv 1 Stellar
         Vortex. The vortex detonates 3 seconds after its creation,
         dealing Cryo damage and applying 1U of Cryo gauge. The base damage
         of a Lv 1 Stellar Vortex explosion is 2.67x the swirl's Anemo
         damage.

  - Vortex leveling & early detonation:
    Only one Stellar Vortex is permitted on the field at a time. If
    additional Swirls occur while a vortex is already active, they empower
    the existing vortex instead of spawning duplicates:
      * Lv 2 Upgrade: If 2 additional Swirls (3 Swirls total) are
        triggered before the vortex explodes, it upgrades to a Lv 2 Stellar
        Vortex. The 3-second detonation timer remains unchanged, but its
        explosion damage is multiplied by 1.5x.
      * Premature Detonation: If 3 further Swirls (6 Swirls total) are
        triggered after upgrading to Lv 2 but before it detonates, the
        vortex explodes prematurely on that 3rd Swirl. This immediately
        frees up the field so a brand-new vortex can spawn on subsequent
        swirls.

  - Multi-Contributor Damage Scaling:
    Both the Swirl and Vortex portions scale with the combined stats of all
    characters who contributed to the reaction. Base damage scales with
    each contributor's character level, their Elemental Mastery, and their
    Crit stats. Relevant Stellar Swirl/Glimmer bonuses and Elevation buffs
    are applied as well.

    The engine calculates reaction damage individually for each
    participating contributor. Each individual contribution rolls for crit
    independently based on that contributor's Crit Rate. The calculated
    damage values are then sorted from highest to lowest and distributed
    according to fixed shares:
      * 1st contributor (highest): 60% of total damage
      * 2nd contributor:           30% of total damage
      * 3rd contributor:            5% of total damage
      * 4th contributor:            5% of total damage

    If fewer than 4 characters contributed, the percentage distribution
    remains identical (e.g. with 2 contributors, shares are 60% and 30%),
    and the remaining unassigned percentage is truncated.
    If the contributor with the highest damage share rolled a crit, the
    reaction as a whole is displayed as a critical hit.

  - Contributor credit rules:
    * Cryo contributors: Every Cryo character who applied or refreshed the
      Cryo aura used by that specific Stellar Swirl is credited as a
      contributor. The credit eligibility window matches the duration of
      the aura (9.5 seconds for a 1U application).
    * Anemo contributors: Every Anemo character who triggered the Swirl
      receives contribution credit.
    * Under these rules, exactly one Anemo character can contribute to the
      initial Anemo Swirl portion (alongside up to three Cryo characters).
      The delayed Cryo Vortex explosion can have up to three Anemo
      contributors and up to three Cryo contributors (disregarding innate
      enemy auras).
    * In the timeline log, credit for the Swirl portion is assigned to the
      triggering Anemo character; credit for the Vortex explosion is
      assigned to the last Anemo character who triggered a Swirl.

  - Why multipliers are hidden for Stellar Reactions:
    Because each of the n contributors can independently crit or not crit,
    evaluating the true average damage requires computing a probability
    distribution across 2^n distinct states. For 4 contributors, that means
    2^4 = 16 states! To display Base DMG, Bonus Mult, Elev Mult, and Crit
    Mult across all 16 states would require 16 * 4 = 64 individual columns
    in the table. I haven't settled on a clean, compact way to present all
    those intermediate numbers yet, so they are omitted from the detailed
    multipliers block for now.

8. SAVING AND LOADING A BUILD
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Found in: Tab 1 (Team & Gear Setup) -> Save / Load Build

  Export Current Build (.json)
      Exports a JSON file containing the full party setup, gear (main
      stats and substats), enemy configuration, custom field sequences,
      rotations, trials, and the currently selected trial.

  Upload Saved Build (.json)
      Restores a previously exported build file, loading all settings into
      the interface while keeping everything fully editable. A loaded file
      applies once upon upload; upload it again if you want to revert back
      to it.

  User Manual (.txt)
      Downloads a copy of this manual file.

Note: You can also export individual runs directly from Tab 4 using the
"Export Run" button, which bundles that specific run's party, gear, and
trial setup.

9. ASSUMPTIONS AND LIMITATIONS
----------------------------------------------------------------------
- Rotations are idealized: abilities' casts hit at the exact timestamps
  specified in your field sequences, and every hit resolves at its fixed
  offset from cast time.
- Persistent hits continue resolving off-field (for example, deployables or
  summons that remain active after swapping). Swapping never cancels an
  already-scheduled hit.
- Energy and particles are not simulated. Energy Recharge is treated solely
  as a stat line.
- Constellation levels that grant talent levels (C3 and C5) are not
  applied automatically; please input your final talent levels directly.
- The enemy is modeled as a single target with static base resistances and
  standard defense formulas.
- Damage values are reported as mathematical averages (Expected Value =
  Crit Rate * Crit DMG), rather than randomized trial runs.
